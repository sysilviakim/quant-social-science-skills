## 1. What the paper establishes

무작위 실험 중 여러 처치를 교차 배정하는 요인 설계는 복수의 처치를 효율적으로 평가할 수 있지만, 상호작용을 처리하는 통계적 관행은 잘못된 추론을 초래할 수 있다. 논문이 지적하는 첫 번째 문제는 상호작용항을 제외한 short model이다. 상호작용효과가 0이 아니라면 short model 계수는 business-as-usual 대비 주효과가 아니라, 다른 처치와의 상호작용 일부가 포함된 복합효과를 추정하게 된다. 이에 따라 주효과 추정치가 달라지고 통상적인 검정의 크기가 왜곡될 수 있다. 지적되는 두 번째 문제는 먼저 long model에서 상호작용의 유의성을 검정하고, 유의하지 않을 때 short model을 선택하는 2단계 절차이다. 이러한 데이터 의존적 모델 선택 과정은 선택 이후 추정량의 분포를 복잡하고 비정규적으로 만들기 때문에, 통상적인 t-검정에 기반한 추론이 유효하지 않다. 또한, 상호작용 검정은 주효과 검정에 비해 훨씬 더 큰 표본을 요구하기 때문에, 상호작용의 비유의성을 상호작용 부재의 증거로 해석해서는 안 된다. 반대로 낮은 검정력에서 유의한 상호작용은 실제 효과의 크기를 과장하는 Type-M error를 일으킬 수 있다. 저자들은 상위 5개 경제학 저널에 게재된 요인설계를 활용한 현장실험 논문 27편을 조사하고, 모든 상호작용을 포함하지 않은 연구 중 공개자료가 있는 논문 15편을 재분석했다. 그 결과, short model에서 5% 수준으로 유의했던 결과 55개 중 29개가 모든 상호작용을 포함한 long model에서 통계적으로 유의하지 않았다. 이를 바탕으로, 저자들은 완료된 실험의 분석에 있어서는 long model을 제시하고, 새로운 실험을 설계할 때에는 주효과가 주요한 관심사라면, 상호작용 셀을 비우고 그 표본을 각 단독처치집단과 통제집단에 재배분하는 것을 권고한다. 상호작용 자체가 주요 관심이라면, 상호작용 검출을 기준으로 충분한 표본을 설계하고 이를 사전분석계획서에 명시해야 한다.

## 2. Checks for a reviewer

**(a) Check whether a paper using a factorial design clearly states what each reported treatment coefficient estimates.** A coefficient from a short model is generally not a main effect relative to a business-as-usual counterfactual. It is a weighted average of treatment effects across other treatment assignments. A paper that refers to this coefficient(from short-model) as a "main effect" without explaining its composite estimand misinterprets the coefficient.

**(b) If the paper reports a short model as an estimate of the business-as-usual main effect, check how any nonzero omitted interaction changes the reported coefficient.** In a 2X2 factorial design, the short-model coefficient equals the business-as-usual main effect + the interaction effect multiplied by the assignment probability of the other treatment. If the interaction is zero, the two coefficients are equal. A positive interaction moves the short-model coefficient upward, while a negative interaction moves it downward.

**(c) Check whether the paper tests the interactions and then uses the short model because they are not statistically significant.** This is a data-dependent model selection procedure. A paper that drops the interactions after testing them cannot use the usual t-statistic based inference as if the final model has been chosen before examining the data. The reviewer should ask for the long-model results for completed experiments regardless of the interaction-test result.

**(d) If the paper evaluates an interaction effect, check whether the experiment has enough power to detect substantively meaningful interaction effects.** Don't accept an insignificant interaction as evidence that the interaction is zero. If an interaction is statistically significant in a low-powered experiment, check whether the paper reports the risk of magnitude exaggeration, or Type-M error. It should report its power to detect interactions, not only its power to detect main effects.

**(e) If the manuscript describes a planned experiment whose data have not yet been collected and whose primary interest is main effects, check whether the design leaves interaction cells empty and assigns more units exclusively to the single-treatment and control groups.** This design-based approach maintains valid inference and provides greater power for detecting main effects than the long model.

## 3. Evidence

**For (a).** The authors first explain that the short and long estimate different treatment effects. They explain that "the long model identifies the main effects relative to a business-as-usual counterfactual, whereas the short model yields a weighted average of treatment effects"(p.9). They then explain, "This view is consistent with how papers we reanalyze motivate their object of interest, which is usually the main treatment effect against a business-as-usual counterfactual."(p.9). Finally they find that the papers using short model generally failed to explain the composite meaning of their coefficients correctly:"we did not find any study that mentioned (in the main text or in a footnote) that the presented treatment effects should be interpreted as either (a) a composite effect"(p.9). These papers generally sought main effects relative to business as usual, but the short model estimated a weighted average, and none explained the composite interpretation.

**For (b).** The authors explain the relationship between short-model coefficient, the main effect and the interaction effect: "βs 1 can be written as the sum of the ATE of T1 relative to a counterfactual where T2 = 0 and the interaction effect multiplied by the fraction of individuals with T2 = 1"(p.8) Equation (9) expresses this relationship as βs1 =β1 + β12P(T2=1)(p.8). When P(T2=1) is nonzero, the equation can imply that a positive interaction moves the short-model coefficient upward, while a negative interaction moves it downward.(This directional conclusion is not a direct statement by the authors, but an algebraic implication of Equation (9).)

**For (c).** The authors identify choosing a model after testing whether the interaction is zero as a problematic procedure. They explain that "the distributions of the estimators obtained from this data-dependent model selection procedure are complicated and highly non-normal, rendering the usual t-statistic-based inference invalid"(p.19) They also say, "It is also important to specify the estimand of interest in advance in a pre-analysis plan, and not justify the short model ex-post based on estimated interactions being insignificant"(p.32). Therefore, they state "our recommendation for the analysis of completed experiments is to always present the long regression model."(p.32)

**For (d).** The paper states that detecting interaction effects requires much more observations because "the power for detecting interaction effects is always lower than the power for detecting main effects."(p.29): "the required sample size for detecting an interaction effect is always larger than the sample size for detecting a main effect of equal magnitude."(p.29) It then identifies the problem with the lack of power: "This lack of power creates two distinct sets of problems. First, it results in non-rejections of the null of zero interactions(even when they may be meaningful)"(p.33) and also states "Second, it leads to significant results being much more likely to be over-estimates"(p.33) So, the authors recommend that "Following Gelman & Carlin (2014), we suggest studies report power to detect interactions (as well as Type-M errors) in their pre-analysis plan."(p.32)

**For (e).** The authors directly recommend a different design when main effects are the primary interest.:"For the design of new experiments, if main effects are of primary interest, we recommend leaving the interaction cells empty and increasing the number of units assigned exclusively to the treatment or the control groups."(p.32) Explaining this, they state the benefit of this recommendation: "This design-based approach naturally controls size and yields notable global power improvements relative to the long model."(p.32)

## 4. Scope

**Applies to:** randomized factorial, or cross-cut experiments in which subjects can receive more than one independent treatment. The paper's formal analysis is focused on two binary treatments, commonly known as "2X2 factorial designs"(p.6). Checks (a),(c),(d) and (e) also extends to factorial designs with more than two treatments. Because the authors state that "The theoretical analysis of Section 2 straightforwardly extends to more complicated factorial designs."(p.28). Check (a) applies to any such paper. It does not raise a concern when the paper correctly interprets the reported coefficients. Check (b) applies only when a 2X2 factorial experiment presents a short-model coefficient as a business-as-usual main effect. It does not apply to the long model or an explicitly targeted composite effect. And check (c) applies when a paper chooses the short model after finding insignificant interactions. It does not apply when the model was specified before examining the data and was not changed after interaction test. The diagnosis of (d) applies when a paper uses interaction significance to make substantive claims. It does not apply when the paper makes no substantive claim about the existence or absence of interactions. Last, check (e) applies only to planned experiments whose data have not yet been collected and whose primary interest is main effects. It does not apply when the experiment is completed or the primary interest is interaction effects.

**Does not directly apply to:** observational studies, quasi-experiments, or regression in which the treatments were not randomly and independently assigned. The paper's causal framework assumes independent random assignment of the treatments(p.6-7). Therefore, we can't directly apply to non-randomized & independent model. The premise requires some subjects to receive both treatments(p.6).

**Does not apply to:** ordinary two or multi-arm experiments in which the treatment conditions are mutually exclusive. These designs don't have joint-treatment cells and therefore don't create the model problem examined in the checks.

**Partial application:** The short model may be reasonable in four settings: exploratory screening of several treatments, focusing on minimizing mean squared error (MSE) criteria, improving external validity and the case of conceptual experiments(pp.4-5). These cases are conditional exceptions, not general approval of the short model. The reviewer should verify that the paper actually satisfies the justification for the relevant exception.

## 5. What failure looks like

In a paper, a factorial experiment includes single-treatment and combined-treatment but its main regression omits the treatment interactions and interprets the resulting short-model coefficients as business-as-usual main effects. The paper does not explain that these coefficients are weighted-average composite effects. A footnote or appendix states that the interactions were tested and then dropped because they were not statistically significant. Neither the main text nor the appendix reports the fully interacted long model, so the readers can't determine whether the omitted interactions move the reported coefficients upward or downward or change their significance. The paper also reports power only for main effects and treats insignificant interaction as evidence for zero, or interprets significant interactions from a low-powered experiment without assessing magnitude exaggeration or Type-M error. For a planned experiment focused on main effects, It is a failure signal that retaining the combined-treatment cells without explaining any consideration.

## 6. Test cases

**SHOULD trigger:** Thébaud, S., & Pedulla, D. S. (2022). When Do Work-Family Policies Work? Unpacking the Effects of Stigma and Financial Costs for Men and Women. Work and Occupations, 49(2), 229-263. This paper uses a factorial survey experiment. For the parental-leave experiment, three wage replacement conditions and five flexibility stigma conditions were "fully crossed, resulting in a 3 × 5 experimental design with 15 cells."(online first version, p.11). Check (a) should be applied to the models. For example, Model 1 of Table 1 estimates the effects of wage-replacement conditions while controlling for flexibility stigma conditions, not including their interactions(p.14). The reviewer should check these coefficients are reported as effects averaged across the assigned stigma conditions, rather than effects relative to one particular business-as-usual condition. Check (d) also applies because the paper sometimes interprets insignificant interactions as evidence of similar effects: "The interaction term is not statistically significant, indicating that the promotion reassurance prime operates similarly for men and women."(p.16)

**Should NOT trigger:** Hainmueller, J. and Hopkins, D.J. (2015), The Hidden American Immigration Consensus: A Conjoint Analysis of Attitudes toward Immigrants. American Journal of Political Science, 59: 529-548. This study independently randomizes nine immigrant attributes in a conjoint experiment(pp.534-535). However, the paper explicitly defines its target estimand as the AMCE, for which "the average is taken over all possible combinations of the other immigrant attributes."(p.537). Thus, the coefficients are intentionally weighted-average composite effects. So the reviewer's checks should not flag the main analysis merely because it does not include every possible interaction.
